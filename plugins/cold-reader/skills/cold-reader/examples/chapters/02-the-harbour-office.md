---
title: The Harbour Office
pov: Dora Penhallow
---

The harbour office sat at the top of the slipway, a brick box with a
flagpole and a window that looked down on every boat in Saltney. Victor
Lusk had the desk under the window. He did not get up when Dora came in.

She put the log on his blotter, open at Tuesday, and pointed.

Lusk read the line without moving his head. "Busy night."

"I wasn't there. That isn't my writing."

"It says Penhallow."

"Anyone can write Penhallow."

He closed the book gently, as if it might bruise, and slid it back
across the desk. "Dora. The log is the log. It goes to the river
inspector every quarter, and the inspector reads what is in it. If a
pilot starts telling him her own entries are wrong, he starts asking
what else in the book is wrong. You see where that goes."

"It goes to whoever wrote it."

"It goes to the licence." He smiled at her. "Your father never had
trouble with his paperwork."

She took the log and went out. On the stairs she passed Annie Croft, the
clerk, coming up with two mugs of tea. Annie stopped and looked at the
book under Dora's arm, and then down the stairs, and then back up at the
office door.

"Tuesday night," Annie said quietly. "There was a lorry on the slip. One
of Hallam's."

"Hallam's gravel?"

"His barges want your mooring. The gravel comes down the river, it has
to come off somewhere." Annie lowered her voice further. "Hallam's
married to Mr Lusk's sister. Twenty years now. I did the flowers."

Dora stood on the stairs with the log against her ribs. Down on the
river a barge was coming in on the last of the flood, low in the water
with its load, and the ferry sat at her mooring with the chain slack
behind it.

"I didn't tell you," Annie said, and went on up with the tea.

Dora walked back down to the ferry. She did not put the log in the tin
box. She put it inside her coat, against the lining, where she could
feel the corner of it every time she breathed.
