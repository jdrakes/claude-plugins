---
title: The First Crossing
pov: Dora Penhallow
---

The chain came up out of the river cold and brown, and Dora Penhallow
felt it through the soles of her boots before she heard it. Six in the
morning, the tide two hours off full, the Cale running wide and slow
under a sky the colour of dishwater.

She had three passengers: a nurse going off shift, a man with a crate of
hens, and a boy on a bicycle who should have been at school. The ferry
was licensed for twelve. Her father had painted the number on the
wheelhouse door himself, in white, and every spring she painted over it
in the same place so the brush strokes stayed his.

On the far bank she let the ramp down, watched the hens go off first,
and went into the wheelhouse to write the crossing in the log. The book
lived in a tin box screwed to the shelf, beside the radio and a jar of
pencils with their ends chewed.

She turned to Monday, then to Tuesday. Tuesday had her last crossing at
half past seven in the evening, in her own square capitals, and then a
line she had not written. Crossing 23:40. One vehicle. Pilot: D.
Penhallow. The letters leaned to the right. Her letters had never leaned
anywhere.

She read it twice. She had been in bed by ten on Tuesday. She remembered
because the wind had got up and rattled the bedroom window, and she had
lain awake listening to it and thinking about the chain.

The boy on the bicycle knocked on the wheelhouse glass. "Are we going
back or what?"

"In a minute."

She ran her thumb over the entry. The pencil was soft, softer than the
ones in the jar, and it had left a shine on the paper. Someone had sat
in her wheelhouse with their own pencil and written her name.

She closed the book, put it back in the tin, and took the ferry across
with the boy and nobody else. The chain came up cold and brown, the same
as before. She did not look at the box again until she had tied up, and
then she looked at it for a long time.
